Guion 46

EN
The most dangerous thing a client can say to you is: no, I love it.
She says it with the hand mirror already going back down on the armrest. She is not being polite about a cut she loves. She is being polite about a cut you finished by guessing, because halfway in you could not remember where the weight was supposed to sit, so you gave her something adjacent and hoped the blow dry would cover it.
It was never the shears. It was never your hands. It is that you were deciding the cut and doing the cut at the same time.
That is what a Cut Map is for. One page per cut: sectioning, elevation, guide section, the numbered sequence and the mistake to avoid. Checked in seconds, mid-service, without digging through a long video.
Twenty-five of them. This time she says it looking straight at it, and she rebooks before she leaves.
Check the page before your first section.

Está siendo amable con un corte que terminaste adivinando, porque a mitad de camino no te acordabas dónde tenía que quedar el peso, le diste algo parecido y confiaste en que el brushing lo tapara.
Nunca fue la tijera. Nunca fueron tus manos. Es que estabas decidiendo el corte y haciendo el corte al mismo tiempo.
Para eso es un Cut Map. Una página por corte: seccionado, elevación, mecha guía, la secuencia numerada y el error a evitar. Chequeado en segundos, en pleno servicio, sin buscar en un video largo.
Veinticinco. Esta vez lo dice mirándolo de frente, y vuelve a reservar antes de irse.
Mirá la página antes de tu primera sección.

EN
Nobody freezes at the start of a haircut. You freeze in the middle, with the shears in the air and her watching you in the mirror.
You know that cut. You have seen it done a dozen times. But knowing a cut and remembering the order of it with a woman already sitting in your chair are two different jobs, and the second one is the only one that pays.
That is not a technique problem. That is a memory problem, and memory is the one thing you can keep on the shelf next to your shears.
Open the map for that cut. Sectioning, elevation, guide section, a numbered sequence and the mistake to avoid, in the order you actually cut in.
Twenty-five cuts, checked in seconds mid-service, on your phone or printed. Less reworking. The hour you used to lose, back.
Open the map before you pick up your shears.

Guion 48

EN
The stylist in your town whose column is full six weeks out is not better than you. She just reads it first.
Ten seconds before she picks up her shears she looks at her phone. Not a tutorial. One page: sectioning, elevation, guide section and the numbered sequence for the exact cut the woman in her chair asked for. Then she puts the phone face down and cuts it like she has done it a hundred times, because on paper she has.
Checking does not make you less of a professional. It is what the ones you quietly measure yourself against have been doing the whole time. They just do it where you cannot see them.
Twenty-five Cut Maps, on your phone or printed, checked in seconds mid-service. The cuts you used to talk women out of go straight back on your menu.
Read it first. Then cut.
